02

Findings

Finding 01

57% of social media users surveyed say brands should prioritize original series

In a 2025 Sprout Social survey of 2,280 social media users in the US, UK and Australia, 57% said brands should prioritize posting original content series in 2026. That answer was nearly tied with the top answer, interacting with audiences.

Two brands that made a series have said publicly what it cost or what it returned. Aéropostale's The Intern Diaries stars creator Déjà Clark, and its second season has six episodes of about three to four minutes each. Marisa Thalberg, the marketing chief at Catalyst Brands, which licenses Aéropostale, told Digiday that the show overall cost "really low six figures". Bob's Discount Furniture launched Till Decor Do Us Part in July 2025, with comedian Gabby Bryan as host and six real couples as contestants, and released a second season in fall 2026.

The results under the two films below are the companies' own figures.

The Intern Diaries · Aéropostale · Season 2, episode 1 · 3 minutes 49 seconds

Marisa Thalberg of Catalyst Brands, to Digiday: "On Instagram, we saw two times the engagement on 'Intern Diaries' content versus our regular content that we program."

Till Decor Do Us Part · Bob's Discount Furniture · Season 1, episode 2 · 1 minute 40 seconds

Company-reported: the first season drove $9 million in attributed sales.

Finding 02

68% of US Google searches in early 2026 produced no click

In the first four months of 2026, 68% of US Google searches ended without a click, according to SparkToro. The company put the 2024 figure at 60%, using a different data provider. Pew Research found that in March 2025, people clicked a traditional search result on 8% of Google search visits that showed an AI summary, against 15% of visits that did not.

In Metricool's 2026 study of 670,000+ LinkedIn posts, posts with links from personal profiles got 27% fewer impressions and 20% fewer interactions than posts without links. Advertising on Meta costs more too. Meta reported that its average price per ad rose 14% year over year in the third quarter of 2026. The price has been higher than a year earlier in each of the seven quarters since the start of 2025.

An episode that a person watches on the platform where they found it does not need the click. Shopify's Make My Space Work is one example. A designer rebuilds one small-business owner's workspace per episode, the business owners on camera are Shopify's customers, and the series sits on a YouTube channel of its own.

Make My Space Work · Shopify · 15 minutes 22 seconds

5.0 million views across the 27 videos on the series channel, read 11 November 2026.

Finding 03

In a US survey, 69% of people who view online content said they trust AI-generated content less

In a YouGov survey of 2,557 US adults commissioned by Pangram in April 2026, 69% of people who view online content said they trust AI-generated content less than human-generated content. Only 8% trust it more. In the same survey, 61% said they are unlikely to read or engage with content they think is AI-generated. Pangram sells AI-detection software, so the company has an interest in the result.

LinkedIn's chief product officer, Hari Srinivasan, wrote in August 2026 that members were seeing "40% less views on what we classify as AI slop" than a few weeks earlier.

Every series embedded in this report puts real people on camera. YETI Presents is the plainest case: 90 short documentaries, each about one person telling a story from their own life. The people in the films are anglers, hunters, cooks, surfers and rodeo riders.

A Thousand Casts, from YETI Presents · YETI · 28 minutes 29 seconds

38.2 million views across the 90 films in the library, read 11 November 2026.

Finding 04

Inter Milan posted about a third less, and the club reports a larger audience

Marketing Brew reported in September 2026 that Inter Milan cut the amount of content it posted by about a third over the past year. Across its channels, followers rose about 20%, impressions 43% and video views 33%, according to the club.

One club's figures do not settle the question, and other data points the other way on followers. In Buffer's data from about 161,000 accounts on Facebook, Instagram and X, accounts posting 10 or more times a week gained an average of 32 more followers a week than in weeks they didn't post. Buffer, which sells scheduling software, also analyzed 15.7 million posts, and that analysis suggests reach per post tends to fall as frequency rises.

Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, said in January 2025 that the top three signals for ranking are watch time, likes and sends.

A series can run on few episodes. Huckberry's DIRT has published 18 episodes in four and a half years, about four a year, and the median episode runs 34 minutes.

New York City, from DIRT · Huckberry · 24 minutes 37 seconds

24.1 million views across 18 episodes published between March 2022 and September 2026.

03

Our own numbers: 19 brand series on YouTube

Talex tracks 19 brand series on YouTube. On 11 November 2026 we read the public figures for all 19 from YouTube's data service. We chose these series because they worked, so they show what a successful brand series looks like and do not prove that series succeed. YouTube's public view counts can include views from paid ads and do not separate them, so no figure here should be read as unpaid viewing.

A series can account for a large share of a channel's viewing. The 33 films in Volvo Trucks' Live Tests make up 67.2% of the lifetime views on the Volvo Trucks channel. The 18 episodes of DIRT make up 40.1% of Huckberry's channel views, IKEA USA's makeover playlist 35.4% of its channel's, and YETI Presents 31.3% of YETI's. The share is not always large. Cat Trials is 7.5% of the views on Caterpillar's product channel, and Crucible Moments is 9.6% of Sequoia Capital's.

Five of the 19 have published for more than ten years. Roadkill, Will It Blend?, Volvo Trucks' Live Tests, Cat Trials and YETI Presents each span more than a decade. We measured the span from the first upload to the latest in the playlist we used.

Seven of the 19 come from companies that sell to businesses. Sequoia Capital's Crucible Moments has 1.95 million views across 21 documentaries in which a company's founder or leaders tell its story, with a median episode of about 42 minutes. Shopify's Make My Space Work has 5.0 million views across 27 videos, with a median of about 13 minutes.

Eight of the 11 longer series have a median episode between 6:55 and 14:49. The 11 are the series whose median episode is longer than five minutes. The other eight series are short, with medians from 58 seconds to 4 minutes 19 seconds. We keep the two groups apart because view counts for short and long episodes are not comparable.

In 11 of the 19 series, no episode title contains the brand's name. That count reflects naming habits as well as intent, because a series such as YETI Presents carries the brand in the series name.

A short self-check

Five questions to ask about a piece of content before it is made.

  1. Tom Langan's test, from his Value First newsletter: "If you stripped your name and your product off this piece of content, would anyone still want to watch it?"
  2. Can the format be described in one sentence, so that a viewer of one episode knows what the next will be?
  3. Is a real person on camera, telling a story that is theirs?
  4. Does the piece hold a viewer on the platform where it is watched, without needing a click?
  5. Could the team still be making it a year from now?
