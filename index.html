Finding 01

Social media users are asking brands for series

Sprout Social asked social media users what brands should prioritize in 2026. Original content series came in second at 57%1, nearly tied with the top answer, interacting with audiences.

One example of a brand giving people what they're asking for is Aéropostale's The Intern Diaries. The series stars creator Déjà Clark and its second season has six episodes of about three to four minutes each. Marisa Thalberg of Catalyst Brands, the company that runs Aéropostale, told Digiday that the show cost "really low six figures"7 to produce.

Bob's Discount Furniture created a series called Till Decor Do Us Part, hosted by comedian Gabby Bryan, and cast six real couples as contestants. The first season drove $9 million in attributed sales8, and a second season was released this fall.

In practice

Plan a first season as a handful of short episodes built around people your audience identifies with and would choose to watch.

Finding 02

Clicks are harder to get, and more expensive

In the first four months of 2026, 68%2 of US Google searches ended without a click, up from 60% in 2024 according to SparkToro. And Pew Research found that when Google showed an AI summary, people clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits, against 15% without one9.

LinkedIn shows a similar pattern. Personal-profile posts with a link got 27% fewer impressions and 20% fewer interactions than the average post5. And paid reach is becoming more expensive. Meta's average price per ad rose 14%3 year over year in the third quarter of 2026, and the price has been higher than a year earlier in every quarter since the start of 2025.

Most branded content is really a detour that only pays off if the viewer clicks on a call to action. A good episodic series isn't a detour, it's the destination and it doesn't rely on a call to action to deliver value to the audience or the brand. In Shopify's Make My Space Work, a designer rebuilds a small-business owner's workspace in each episode, on a budget. By the end of the show, the audience has seen that Shopify is a brand that shows up for entrepreneurs and small-business owners without ever having to click through.

In practice

Treat the show as something people would choose to watch, not as an ad they have to put up with. Before you greenlight an episode, ask whether it would still be worth watching with your brand removed. If the answer is no, it's an ad.

Finding 03

Viewers trust human-made content more than AI-generated content

In a YouGov survey of US adults, 69%4 of people who view online content said they trust AI-generated content less than human-generated content. In the same survey, 61%4 said they are unlikely to read or engage with content they think is AI-generated.

LinkedIn's chief product officer, Hari Srinivasan, wrote in August 2026 that members were seeing "40% less views on what we classify as AI slop"10 than a few weeks earlier.

A series like YETI Presents is the kind of human-made content people say they trust more. Each of the 90 documentary episodes features a real person telling a story from their life.

In practice

Build each episode around a real person telling their stories.

Finding 04

Posting less can still grow an audience

Inter Milan cut the content it posted by about a third11 over the past year. Across the club's channels, followers rose about 20%, impressions 43% and video views 33%.

This essentially boils down to quality over quantity. In our view, two good posts a week will earn more engagement than twenty mediocre posts. And for a branded series, the math favors sharing one episode that is worth watching over five episodes designed to fill a content calendar.

And, a series can run on few episodes. Huckberry's DIRT has published just 18 episodes in four and a half years, about one every quarter, with a median length of 34 minutes. And DIRT has an audience of devoted fans eagerly anticipating each new episode.

In practice

Choose a production schedule and release pace your team can maintain for a year.

Our Own Numbers

19 brand series on YouTube by the numbers

Talex Media tracked 19 brand series on YouTube, all showing what success looks like. YouTube view counts can include paid views.

One series can account for most of a channel's views.

Volvo Trucks' 33 Live Tests films make up 67.2%6 of all views on their channel. DIRT is 40.1%6 of Huckberry's and YETI Presents 31.2%6 of YETI's. Cat Trials is 7.4%6 of Caterpillar's product channel.

Five of the 19 span more than a decade.

The five series are Roadkill, Will It Blend?, Live Tests, Cat Trials and YETI Presents6.

Companies that sell to businesses make series too.

Seven of the 19 are B2B brands6. Sequoia Capital's Crucible Moments has 1.96 million views across 21 documentaries of about 42 minutes each at the median6.

Most of the longer series run about 7 to 15 minutes an episode.

Of the 11 series with a median episode over five minutes, eight have a median between 6:55 and 14:496.

Most of the series keep the brand out of the episode title.

In 11 of the 19, no episode title contains the brand's name6.

Predictions

Branded episodic content keeps growing.

Sprout Social’s 2026 research puts episodic series as marketers’ number one social priority for the year12, and branded content studios averaged 18% year-over-year growth13.

Brands become entertainers again.

Procter & Gamble invented soap operas back in the 1930s14, and audiences want that kind of relationship back: 73% of people say brands should make more effort to be entertaining15.

Brands build content studios.

59% of brands now produce their video in-house according to Wyzowl16. But 92% of large brands with in-house agencies still bring in outside partners to scale production17. More mid-sized and smaller brands will integrate independent production companies with their marketing teams to create fractionalized content studios.

Vertical and scripted series keep growing.

Microdrama revenue is projected to reach $14 billion globally by the end of 2026, including $1.5 billion in the US18, and active advertisers on microdrama apps grew 132%19.

Comedians become media partners.

YouTube is piloting a program for brands to sponsor creator shows, and Google says brands can already integrate into creator programming that includes Trevor Noah’s stand-up special20. Next come brands that fund whole comedy specials and other comedy or variety entertainment formats.

Independent media gains ground.

YouTube reached a record 15.8% of TV viewing in August, about twice Netflix’s share21. US streamers nearly quadrupled their new creator-led programming in a year, and 94% of it came from independent production companies22.

AI floods the feed, and human-made series become the premium.

Overseas, AI-generated and animated dramas are expected to account for nearly 70% of the microdrama market in 202623. Forrester predicts that in 2027 human-generated content will become a top priority again for 50% of brands24.

Self-Check

Five questions before you make your series

  1. If you stripped your name and your product off this piece of content, would anyone still want to watch it?
  2. Can you describe the format in one sentence, so a viewer of one episode knows what to expect from the series?
  3. Can your audience relate to or identify with the people on camera?
  4. Is the show the destination or is it a detour that requires additional action from your audience?
  5. Could your team keep up the production and release schedule for a year?
